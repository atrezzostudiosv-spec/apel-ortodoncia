Conscious Sedation

If you are a patient with great fear of the dentist, do not stop treating your mouth, as at our Apel Clinic we offer a service to suit you. Conscious sedation, a procedure where an anesthesiologist, gives you a medication so that you feel completely comfortable while undergoing dental care, so you will have no feeling of anxiety and no memory of your visit as you leave the clinic. The conscious sedation procedure at the dental clinic aims to achieve a patient's anxiety monitoring status by controlled depression of the central nervous system. It's called conscious sedation, because the patient's capable of breathing normally and of responding to all the cheeses and verbal commands requested. That procedure, as well as being beneficial to the patient, also facilitates the work of the dentist, who can usually conduct dental care more quickly, while the patient is undergoing conscious sedation. This conscious sedation procedure at the dental clinic is performed by an anesthetic doctor, who will supervise the patient at all times while the dentist conduct dental care.

In dentists, the conscious sedation procedure, usually referred to as:

1. Extent fear of dentist

2. Intensive dental care or procedures that require many hours in the dental chair.

In cases with a dentist's panic, this therapy is very positive for the patient, as he doesn't get about anything that's been done with him, so that he's completely relaxed, and he doesn't remember his procedure, so his way out of his dental clinic with a sense of quiet that will help to some extent overcome his fear of his dentist. With respect to very long-term care, the use of conscious sedation at the dental clinic will allow the dentist to advance much of his treatment without the patient's tiredness of his or her being so long with his or her mouth open.

Other indications for conscious sedation procedure at dental clinic

1. Psychic impairment patients

For patients with difficulties with adapting to dental care, this procedure does not only make it easier for the dentist to be more quickly and effectively treated, but to some extent the level of suffering or stress that dental care can generate for these patients is reduced.
