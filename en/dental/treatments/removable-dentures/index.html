Dental Protheses

The dental prosthetic is a device designed to replace lost teeth. Teeth are a very important part of our appearance. If we're missing a tooth, we're already discouraged and insecure about ourselves. What if we lose more? If we leave our mouth without teeth, we run the risk of bad occlusion and a number of other adverse conditions and physical changes, certainly. To avoid these situations, you have to go to the technician that will help you choose the right dental prosthetic for all situations.

Protheses

Depending on the patient's need, we can find different types:

Reportable complete prostheses:

The complete prostheses are those with no remaining teeth in the mouth and cover the whole surface of the bone crest, that is to say the bone where the teeth were previously found. Due to their type of restraint they use, they are called muchosupported prostheses, with this meaning that they are held up in their mouth by the gums and internal anatomy of the prosthetic, which have been performed after faithfully copying the anatomy of the patient, through models that provide exactly how the elderage will be supported. The market has an infinity of creams and pads that help make this type of prosthetic more attached. One of the benefits mentioned above is aesthetics. A patient who cannot conduct an implant prosthetic will have to go without teeth. With this type of prosthetic you can always have teeth and choose the color, shape and alignment that best suits your aesthetic needs. Today this type of prosthetic are usually made with a system called "by induction," that provides, with respect to the old system, more resistance, both to blows and to wear by time. If however the patient wants a strengthening of his or her prosthetic, there's always an option to place a metal mesh at the palate area.

Reportable partial prostheses of resin:

Resin remodeling prostheses are indicated for patients with some teeth at the arcade and cover the dentate area. By the type of clamp these are called muchodenosupported. They are attached to their mouths through the gum and also through the remaining teeth of the arcade, thanks to hooks that hold the natural tooth through the area of his neck. These hooks can sometimes be visible and so there are so-called "esthetic hooks." They are of the same color as the tooth or even the color of the gum, depending on its location. They provide more aesthetics but with the drawback that they are something more fragile than conventional hooks. For the structure of the prosthetic in very similar to that of the complete prosthetic, even with the option to place a metal mesh as a reinforcement but with consideration to its design. The esthetic will depend basically on the remaining teeth of the arcade. In this type of prosthetic a harmony with the natural teeth is sought, meaning that the color, shape and size of the new teeth will be equal to the natural ones. That's how they have been put up with nylon. These are more flexible prostheses that are more easily adapted to the oral cavity with the material with which they are made (Nylon). They have some hooks made from the same material from the base of the prosthetic, that are pink, to "camouflage" with the gums and to disguise their presence. That type of prosthetic is relatively new and most of them have been used exclusively for a given time, that is as a provisional prosthetic.

Reportable or skeletal partial prosthetic:

Skeletal prostheses, such as partial resin prostheses, are indicated in patients with an area or several denatured areas at the arcade but with some remaining teeth at the arcade. The most important feature of this type of prosthetic is that its structure is made from different metal alloys. That gives the prosthetic more resistance to chewing and wear. They have several metal connectors that are united with each other with the main base of the structure, providing better stability to the different types of forces to which the prosthetic can be submitted during chewing and speech and with insertion and removal. That type of prosthetic will always be made up of hooks, which as we have mentioned earlier, can be camouflaged into the oral cavity and put them from the color of the tooth or the color of the gum. One of the ways to achieve more stability of the prosthetic without hooks, is with the use of Ataches or Connectors. That's two, a male and a female. The male connector is placed at the removable prosthetic, at the area where it will be in direct contact with the natural teeth and the female connector at the remaining tooth that will be in direct contact with the apparatus. To put this connector onto the tooth, it should have a metal-porcelain crown. To have the attacks work properly, they have to be put at the far end of the remaining teeth at both sides of the mouth. Thus, there will be a proper balance in the attachment of the prosthetic. Removeable prostheses are the solution for patients who do not wish or cannot use dental implants.
